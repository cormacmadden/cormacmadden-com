Motion Picture Engineering assignment: decide which bitrate each rendition of a streaming ladder should use.

## How it works

A Python script drives FFmpeg for every combination of resolution (180p, 360p, 720p) and bitrate (64 Kbps to 3 Mbps). Each clip is encoded to H.264 with the `slow` preset, decoded back to raw YUV, upscaled to 720p and compared with the original using PSNR and SSIM. The results become rate–distortion curves, and the points where one resolution overtakes the next give the crossover bitrates, about 102 Kbps and 925 Kbps.

## What I chose

- **180p at 96 Kbps (28.5 dB).** 360p scores higher at this bitrate but its block artefacts are far more distracting, the numbers and the eye disagree.
- **360p at 612 Kbps (39 dB).** Comparable to 720p at the crossover point.
- **720p at 3 Mbps (~44 dB).** The gain over 2 Mbps is small, but for a high-value feature film the extra bandwidth is worth it.
