## Context

A car configurator is a familiar brief: one hero car, a handful of paints and environments, an interior you can look around, and everything running in real time. What usually makes it expensive is not the rendering but the setup work in between.

So I gave myself a weekend. The goal was to see how far an Unreal Engine 5 configurator could get from a standing start in two days if AI took on as much of the slow, repetitive part of the workflow as possible, and I kept the decisions about how it should look.

## Where AI took the time out

- **Retargeting animations.** Moving animations onto a new rig is fiddly, rule-based work, the kind that is easy to describe and slow to do by hand.
- **Variant preparation.** Every paint and environment option has to be set up and kept consistent across the scene. AI handled the bulk of that preparation so the variants could be switched on one car.
- **Prototyping.** Templates, layouts and background examples were generated quickly, so I could try ideas and throw away the weak ones in minutes rather than hours.

## The result

A working configurator and the stills on this page, all rendered in Unreal Engine 5: exterior and overhead shots, and the details that make an F1 an F1, down to the central driving seat.

The point is less the car than the workflow. The parts of real-time visualisation that used to eat the schedule are the parts AI is now good at, which leaves the time for the parts that make it look good.
